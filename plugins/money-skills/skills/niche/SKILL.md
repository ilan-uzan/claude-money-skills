# /niche: 10 niches, 1 rose

A dating show where the user is choosing who to build a business for. The
niche has to be a group of people, not a topic.

## Step 1: getting to know you (ask, then wait)

Ask in one message and wait:

1. What do you know how to do? (Skills, job, hobbies, things you have fixed.)
2. Who do you like spending time with or helping?
3. What do you already sell, or want to sell? (Can be "not sure".)
4. Who do you know already? (Industries, communities, groups you belong to.)
5. Anyone you do not want to work with?

## Step 2: the contestants

Write 10 niches. Each is "[specific people] who [specific situation]", for
example "new physiotherapists opening their first clinic". Each uses something
the user said. One line on why they might need the user.

## Step 3: the rounds

**Round 1, first impressions (10 → 6).** Cut 4 that have no clear painful
problem the user can solve. One line each.

**Round 2, can they pay? (6 → 3).** Cut 3 that cannot or will not pay, or are
too hard to reach. One line each.

**Round 3, the dates (3 → 1).** For each of the last 3, a panel of 4 Claudes
gives a score 0-10 and one line:

- **The Matchmaker**: how well it fits the user's skills and people.
- **The Wallet**: willingness and ability to pay.
- **The Map**: where these people gather (name the kind of place: a
  subreddit type, a trade group, a Facebook group, a conference), and how hard
  they are to reach.
- **The Long Term**: will the user still want this in a year?

## Step 4: the final rose

The winner, with the total score and why it beat second place. Then:

- The one-sentence offer for this niche.
- **The 7-day test**: find 10 real people in this niche, have 5 short
  conversations, and the exact question to ask ("what's the most annoying part
  of X?"). What answer means "go" and what means "try the runner-up".

## Rules

- No made-up market sizes. Say "check this" when something needs research.
- Niches must be legal and ethical; no targeting vulnerable people with
  money promises.
- If nothing fits well, say so and ask one question that would help.
