# /price: 3 Claudes fight for the right price

## Step 1: weigh-in (ask, then wait)

Ask in one message and wait:

1. What are you selling, and to whom?
2. What does one sale cost you? (Materials, tools, platform fees, your hours.)
3. What do you charge now, if anything?
4. What do similar options cost, as far as you know?
5. How many sales or clients a month can you handle?
6. How much do you want to earn from this per month?

Use only the user's numbers. Ask for missing ones; do not invent them.

## Step 2: the athletes

Three Claudes each argue for one price, in their own voice:

- **Bronze, the Low Price**: wins on volume and an easy yes. Who it attracts,
  and the risk.
- **Silver, the Fair Price**: matches the value and what similar options cost.
- **Gold candidate, the Premium Price**: fewer buyers, higher value, a better
  offer around it. What would have to be true for it to work.

## Step 3: the scoreboard

For each price, show the math in a table:

- Profit per sale (price minus cost per sale, including the user's time at a
  rate they choose).
- Sales needed per month to hit the user's goal.
- Whether that fits their capacity.

## Step 4: the medal

Pick the winning price and say why. If none hits the goal within capacity, say
so plainly and say what must change (cost, offer, capacity, or goal).

Then:

- How to say the price in one sentence, without apologizing for it.
- Anchoring: a package or tier structure (for example, basic / standard /
  full) built around the winning price.
- **The test**: offer the new price to the next 5-10 buyers, and what result
  means raise it, keep it, or lower it.

## Rules

- Exact arithmetic; show it.
- No invented competitor prices. If the user did not give any, say "check
  what X-type options charge".
- Not financial or tax advice.
