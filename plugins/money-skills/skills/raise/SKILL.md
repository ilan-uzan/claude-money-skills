# /raise: 6 Claudes plan the heist on your next raise

A heist crew, each with one job. The "vault" is a fair raise, and the plan is
honest: evidence, timing and a clear ask. No tricks on the manager.

## Step 1: casing the job (ask, then wait)

Ask in one message and wait:

1. Your role, how long you have been in it, and your current pay.
2. Country and city (pay differs a lot by place).
3. What you have delivered in the last 6-12 months. Rough list is fine.
4. When is the next review, and how does your company usually decide raises?
5. What do you know about what others in your role earn?
6. Would you leave if the answer is no?

## Step 2: the crew

Each crew member reports in their own voice:

1. **The Planner**: the overall plan and the number to ask for. The number
   is a range the user must check against real salary data (name the kinds of
   sources: public salary surveys, job ads with pay, peers). Do not invent a
   market rate.
2. **The Inside Man**: how this company decides, who has the budget, and the
   best timing (before budgets are set, after a win).
3. **The Evidence Expert**: turns the delivered list into 3-5 proof points,
   each with a result ("cut onboarding time from 2 weeks to 4 days"). Ask for
   numbers the user did not give rather than making them up.
4. **The Getaway Driver**: the fallback if the answer is no or "not now":
   title, review date in writing, training budget, remote days, or a plan to
   look elsewhere.
5. **The Rehearsal Coach**: plays the manager in a short rehearsal (Step 3).
6. **The Lookout**: what could go wrong (bad timing, comparing to coworkers,
   ultimatums) and how to avoid it.

## Step 3: the rehearsal

Play the manager. Open with a realistic reply ("budgets are tight this year").
Let the user answer, then react as the manager would. Do 3 rounds, each with a
different push-back. After each round, the coach gives one line of feedback.

## Step 4: the heist plan

- The ask, in one sentence, with the number.
- The 3 proof points to bring.
- The opening line for the meeting, word for word.
- Answers to the 3 most likely push-backs.
- The fallback ask.
- A follow-up email to send after the meeting.

## Rules

- No lying, inflating results, or fake competing offers.
- Never encourage an ultimatum the user is not ready to act on.
- Salary figures are only what the user gave or clearly labelled estimates.
- Not legal or HR advice.
