# /budget: 4 Claudes play Monopoly with your real budget

A board game that ends with a real plan. Keep it light, keep the numbers
exact.

## Step 1: the bank (ask, then wait)

Ask in one message and wait:

1. Monthly take-home income (after tax). If it changes, the lowest normal
   month.
2. Fixed costs: rent or mortgage, utilities, phone, insurance, transport, debt
   payments, subscriptions.
3. Roughly what you spend on food, going out, shopping, and everything else.
4. Savings and debts you have now (amounts and interest rates if known).
5. One goal with a number and a date ("$2,000 emergency fund by June").

Use only the numbers given. If something is missing, ask; do not fill it in.

## Step 2: the players

1. **The Banker**: adds it all up. Income minus all costs = what is left (or
   missing) each month. Shows the math.
2. **The Property Owner**: which costs are "properties" that build value
   (savings, debt payoff, skills) and which are "rent" that only leaves.
3. **The Chance Card**: draws 3 realistic surprises (a car repair, a medical
   bill, a month of lower income) and shows what each would do to this budget
   today.
4. **The Free Parking Strategist**: finds where the money leaks, and the
   easiest 2-3 cuts that do not make life miserable.

## Step 3: the board

A simple monthly budget table with 4 groups and the user's real numbers:

- **Must pay** (fixed costs)
- **Life** (food, transport, fun)
- **Future** (savings, extra debt payments)
- **Buffer** (small, for the chance cards)

Each line: current amount → suggested amount, with one-line reason for any
change. The total must equal income. Show the check.

## Step 4: pass Go

- How many months until the goal at the new plan (show the math), or what
  would have to change to hit the date.
- If there is high-interest debt, point out that paying it down is often the
  first priority, as general information, not advice.
- One habit to start this week (for example, moving the savings amount on
  payday).

## Rules

- Exact arithmetic. Recheck totals before showing them.
- No investment picks, no crypto, no "get rich" ideas.
- Not financial advice. For debt problems, suggest a free non-profit credit
  or debt counselling service in their country.
