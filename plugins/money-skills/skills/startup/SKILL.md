# /startup: 12 startup ideas enter the arena

## Step 1: tributes from your life (ask, then wait)

Ask in one message and wait:

1. What can you build or do yourself? (Code, design, sales, an industry you
   know.)
2. Problems you or people around you keep running into.
3. Ideas you already have, if any (they enter the arena too).
4. Time and money you can put in over the next 3 months.
5. Solo, or with a co-founder?

## Step 2: the 12 tributes

List 12 ideas, including all of the user's own. Each: one sentence, who has the
problem, and how it makes money. At least half must come from problems the user
named.

## Step 3: the games

**Round 1, the problem (12 → 8).** Is the problem real, frequent and painful?
Cut 4, one line each.

**Round 2, the buyer (8 → 5).** Is there a specific person who would pay, and
who is the decision maker? Cut 3.

**Round 3, the build (5 → 3).** Can the user build a first version in their
time and budget? Cut 2.

**Round 4, the arena (3 → 1).** For the final 3, four Claudes score 0-10
each:

- **The Gamemaker**: is the timing right? What has changed that makes this
  possible now?
- **The Rival District**: who is already doing this, and what is the edge?
  Name competitors only if you are sure they exist; otherwise say "search for
  existing tools that...".
- **The Sponsor**: how do the first 100 users arrive without paid ads?
- **The Mentor**: does this founder have an unfair advantage here?

## Step 4: the victor

The winner, the scores, and why it beat second place. Then a 2-week test that
comes before building anything:

- Days 1-3: talk to 10 people who have the problem. The 5 questions to ask.
- Days 4-7: a one-page description or mock-up, shown to them.
- Days 8-14: ask for something real (a pre-order, a letter of intent, a
  waitlist sign-up with an email).
- What result means "build it", and what means "go back to the arena".

## Rules

- No invented market sizes, funding stories or statistics.
- Ideas must be legal and must not rely on deceiving users.
- Honest: if the user's favourite idea loses, say why plainly.
